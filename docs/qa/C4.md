## Rules checked (plan section 2)

- **Rule 3 (money):** integer minor units everywhere; interest and compensation in `BigInt`, rounded once; no float.
- **Rule 4 (legal text):** the app writes no law. A law is cited only if approved and its conditions hold (C1's `lawApplies`); every legal sentence is either the contract's, the law's own, or one of two fixed next steps, all built by code; the model can't add one (a check, not just the prompt). The real `legal/statutes.json` is empty and every rate is unapproved, so no real letter cites a law or calculates interest yet. "This is not legal advice" is shown to the freelancer on the card, the button and the builder.
- **Rule 5 (nothing sends without approval):** the final demand is an Outbox draft, never auto-approved (the scheduler's auto-send is Tier 1 only), and a test proves nothing is sent before approval.
- **Rule 1 (untrusted text):** no client text reaches the letter's prompt. The facts are database values already formatted.
- **Rule 8 (service role) and rule 9 (logging):** not used; the draft event logs the reminder id and whether a model or the template wrote it, never the letter.
- **`.claude/rules/prompts.md`:** the prompt returns JSON matching the existing draft schema, uses only the facts it is given, treats nothing a client wrote as instruction, and the stored draft carries the "not legal advice" flag.
- **Section 11 Don'ts:** no law cited that isn't approved or whose conditions aren't met; no AI writing of laws, rates or calculated amounts; nothing sent without approval.

## Found and fixed during QA

- The first version let an Outbox edit or a Telegram reword change a final demand, which would have been a way round the checks (an edit is checked as a Tier 3 reminder, which has no interest wording). Both are now refused with a plain message, and the Edit button is hidden.
- A sent final demand would have counted as a past reminder in the scheduler's tier history. It is excluded, so Tier 3 repeats as before.
- Two of my tests were wrong, not the code (a statement with no digit to change, and a capital letter); fixed.
